death (or life)
----

An investigation to how people deal with death, esp. unforseen, premature deaths and suicides.

- General attitude towards death --> something to be avoided if possible; efforts to prolong life...
  - fear of death (in a broader sense) leads to competition (e.g. for living resources) which is essentially violence and is sad to see
  - MBh: Yudhishthira answers the question: What is the greatest wonder of life? (wonder as in "hard to make sense of")\
    > ahany ahani bhūtāni\
      gacchantīha yamālayam\
      śeṣāḥ sthāvaram icchanti\
      kim āścaryam ataḥ paraṁ

    Many many beings meet their deaths yet the foolish thinks he is immortal, what is more wonderful(*) than that?
- Then we have various accounts of what death actually is, biologically, religiously... 
- But one thing is that death is part of life's definition, I cannot imagine life without death
- But then why do people (including me) shun away from deaths, isn't (normal) death something to be celebrated like life itself?
- The aversion to death is maybe related to how we don't want to separate from people/things we love, or the (perceived) reality we are familiar with. For example,
if someone I know (doesn't need to be close) suddenly dies, for me there will be confusion and rejection. People feel like they are living in security and they have plans and are in control and all that, but that's obviously not how life works. I just recently realised that I don't have control over almost anything in my life including my own heart/mind/brain, let alone other things. It's just these types of news (that someone, especially an acquaintance, dies) constantly remind us that we are mortal beings and we may just wake up to death as well. And it immediately poses another existential question, like why am I here at all? These are maybe fundamental questions to life but the way we live, we just sweep them under the carpet and do whatever is expected from us, and finally find out that those who asks us to do things sometimes doesn't know better. It is only when we see/remember our own death as imminent/in due course (vanitas) will we realise that the life we lived was no life, or that we have wasted so much time and energy on useless things (worries, for example). The things we cared so much are no longer worthy, and those we should care and make time for are constantly neglected.
